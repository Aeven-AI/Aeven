# Agent Note: The agent pointer in the browser pane

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-10-01-sidebar-browser-agent-pointer.zh.md)

## Problem

The right sidebar's browser pane draws the agent's pointer over the guest. Four properties had no single owner and each failed visibly. The pointer aimed at an element's **left edge** for chrome controls, because the one targeting rule in the pane existed for the address field, where the typed text starts at the field's left; on a button that put the arrow left of what it pressed. A popup painted **over** the pointer: the three-dot menu mounts as a portal, and its backdrop blur dimmed and smeared the arrow behind the menu. The chrome controls **could not be pressed at all** through the app's bridge — `reload`, `back` and `forward` were rejected as unknown operations, so the pointer that shows an action had no way to reach the buttons it was meant to press. And the pointer **outlived the layout it was placed on**: a page that scrolled under a resting pointer left the arrow where the element had been, and a target-less scroll started from a stale record of the pointer's position, so the arrow entered the page at the viewport's middle rather than from where the reader last saw it.

## Decision

**Aiming is by target kind.** A button or a link takes the centre of its box, on both axes. The address field alone takes its left edge plus `ADDRESS_TEXT_INSET_PX`, because the address is text that begins there. The pane's `centreOf` (elements) and `leftOf` (the address field) name the two cases, so the rule is legible at the call site instead of living in a comment. `CURSOR_TIP_OFFSET_PX` compensates the overlay's own half-box convention — the overlay centres the pointer's box on the point it is given, while the drawn arrow's tip is the box's top-left corner — so the tip lands on the point the pane named. The same offset applies to guest coordinates the shell reports, which are element centres.

**The pointer's layer lives in the page's top layer.** It is portaled to `BODY` as `position: fixed` with `z-index: 1300`, and its box is written from the pane's rectangle on mount, on window resize, and on scroll. Portaling is the requirement, not a preference: the menu is a `position: fixed; z-index: 1100` portal directly under `BODY`, so it is a sibling of the entire pane subtree, and no z-index inside that subtree can paint above it. A popup with `backdrop-filter` blurs everything painted below it, which is what made the pointer dim and smeared.

**A chrome control is named, not positioned by the shell.** The cursor payload carries `action: 'control'` with a `control` name (`back`, `forward`, `refresh`, `capture`, `more`, `zoom-in`, `zoom-out`, `zoom-reset`, or `navigate` for the address field). The shell names the control; the pane finds it, places the pointer on its centre, and clicks it. Controls are looked up in the pane's own chrome row first, then among visible elements anywhere in the document, because the three-dot menu's rows are portaled out of the pane. The press waits for the pointer's arrival, but never longer than the press itself should take: a window the reader has put behind another one throttles animation frames, and a press conditioned on that animation would never fire. A chrome action also clears the shell's record of the pointer's page position, because the pointer leaves the page for a row that is not in it.

**A page that moves puts the pointer back on its element.** The guest reports its own scrolls: the desktop adds a CDP binding to each guest and installs a reporter into every document — `Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument` covers the documents created after it, and each navigation installs the reporter into the document that navigation opens, because an install that ran between two documents would otherwise leave a binding nothing calls. The host window forwards each report to the pane, which re-measures the element it is on and places the pointer on that element, throttled while the movement continues and once more after it stops. Each step of a scroll the shell performs is announced as `action: 'scroll'`, which is the pane's signal that the pointer is placing itself; re-aiming through a gesture fights the gesture's own placement. A target-less scroll records the point the pointer came to rest at, so the next one starts its gesture from where the pointer is.

**The pane draws the pointer and nothing else.** The overlay is attached with `decorations: false`, so it creates no box and draws no click ring, click flash, or typing caret. The page's own reaction is what shows an action landing, and a mark the agent leaves on a page the reader is reading is not the overlay's to add. What survives is the pointer's own motion: it travels to what it acts on, presses it, and holds there until its idle fade.

## Alternatives considered

- **Raising the pane's z-index** above the menu: the menu is a sibling of the pane, so this hides the menu behind the pane's content instead of lifting the pointer over the menu.
- **Keeping the pointer layer inside the pane** and giving it a larger z-index: measured to fail — the whole pane subtree loses to the menu's own layer, whatever the descendant carries.
- **Waiting for the overlay's arrival before pressing**: measured to fail with the window behind another, where the animation never advances.
- **Aiming controls at their left edge**, inheriting the address field's rule: measured at −16 px on the horizontal axis, which is the offset the reader reported as the pointer sitting left of the button.
- **Ringing the element the pointer acts on**: the ring was drawn and measured exactly onto its element, and removed at the reader's request — it marks a page the reader is reading, and the pointer's own position already says what it is acting on.
- **Re-aiming the pointer while the shell performs a scroll**: measured as a fight — the pane placed the pointer back on its element between the gesture's own steps, so the pointer lagged the page it was scrolling.
- **Having the pane poll the guest's scroll offset**: rejected — either a measurement across the bridge on every frame, or a guessed interval, where the page itself can report the moment it moves.

## Consequences

The pointer belongs to the window rather than to the pane, so any change to the pane's geometry must re-place its box; the pane owns that synchronization. Each pressed control needs a `data-control` name, and a control without one cannot be reached by the pointer. The address field stays the single aiming exception. The overlay's half-box convention and the pane's tip offset are two halves of one rule and have to change together. Re-aiming depends on two installs a guest can refuse: a debugger-attached binding and a per-document reporter. A guest that cannot take them reports the failure to the app's own log, and its pointer then stays where the last action put it; nothing else degrades. Each reported scroll costs the pane one measurement of the anchored element, and a page scrolling continuously is measured on the pane's throttle rather than once per report. Only the pointer's own motion is drawn, so the overlay's box, ripple, flash, and caret stay uncreated for this pane and a caller wanting them must attach an overlay without `decorations: false`.

## Verification

Measured live in the running Electron app, as the pointer tip against its target: refresh `−1,−1` with the guest reloading, back `−1,−1`, forward `−1,−1`, capture `0,0`, change-url with the tip on the address text start and the page loading, and an in-page link click at `0,0`. The zoom sequence reads `100% → 110% → 125% → 110%` with the tip on each control. A page scrolled by 160 px moves the anchored element by 160 px and the pointer by 159 px, leaving a 1 px delta, and the pane receives one report per scroll (`reports: 1` for a 90 px scroll). After a press recorded the pointer at `58,107` in the guest's own pixels, a target-less scroll of 200 px moved it to `58,157` — its own horizontal position plus a 50 px drift — where the viewport's middle is 318; a chrome action then cleared the in-page record, and the next target-less scroll entered at `58,74`, that row's entry inset plus the same drift, with a second scroll continuing to `58,124`. With `decorations: false` the overlay's root holds only the pointer layer, the pointer, and its sprite, and the counts of box, label, ripple, flash, and caret nodes are zero while a press lands. The pointer drawn crisp over the open menu is the evidence for the portaled layer. `@aeven/experimental-browser-overlay`'s suites pass 90/90.
